VM failure mitigation: where the evidence stands

From Raghav Gupta, for the advisor, 2 November 2026, ahead of the CSE panel

The 30 pre-registered wins stand, but BAM's saving against never migrating now depends on the assumed migration cost. Placement, not migration, has become the stronger result. G3 needs your view on what the paper should lead with.

Decisions needed from you

  1. G3 and the headline. I recommend leading with the condition and with placement, and reporting BAM's win over the threshold without using it as the main evidence that ranking pays.
  2. Post-freeze work. I recommend a clearly separate exploratory section for the avoidance, law and new-policy results. None of it enters the 30-comparison family.
  3. Cost range sources. Is 4 of 6 enough? Liu 2011 and the SoCC 2015 paper are unread because the ACM site showed a bot challenge. Library access would close this.

Why BAM collapses

This came from a Week 20 robustness flag: "steep" tier weights made BAM collapse, which looked like it contradicted the claim that spread drives the gain. It does not. Those weights raised the cost level as well as the spread. With the same 1:10 ratio and the top weight kept at 1, BAM improves (A3 +17.2%, B1 +6.7%, dev seeds). With the same ratio as default but every weight multiplied by 3.33, it collapses.

So the trigger is failure cost being large against migration cost. A dev-seed test (five seeds, descriptive only) allowed BAM to move each VM only once:

B1P4P4, one move per VMP4-net
Cheap migration−15.8%+3.2%+10.0%
High cost level−75.6%−2.6%+10.6%

Moving the same VMs repeatedly explains most of the collapse. First moves that ignore destination risk explain the rest.

What still does not work

  • P4-net does not fix noise. At σ = 1 it is −18.4% in B1 and −14.8% in B2. At the expensive end it adds nothing. Migration policies stay fragile under noise, while placement policies stay positive at every noise level run.
  • The σ results use two noise definitions. The ADR-013 tests redraw noise every 30 days. The ADR-011 test redraws it daily, which is much harsher: BAM in B1 at σ = 1 is −30.5% under the first and −248% under the second, and avoidance on 2% of hosts falls to +3.8% under daily noise. P4-net and per-tier closing were not run under daily noise, so these numbers should not be compared across the two.
  • Per-tier closing is mainly a rejection result. The saving gain is under 2 points, and in B1 it cannot be told from zero against redirecting without tiers. Rejections are about 0.03% of arrivals at most. It rejects fewer than never migrating does (66 against 169), and I have not explained why.
  • Migration on top of per-tier closing adds nothing measurable (B1 +17.4 against +17.5%).
  • The law has one fitted constant, so it is a regularity, not a derivation. Its extension to tiers missed by 1.7 and 2.1 points in opposite directions. Shuffled scores still leave +2.13% in B1, not fully explained.
  • The guard P6 stays below never migrating in all 12 cost-range cells.
  • Novelty is claimed over the three base papers only. I ran three searches. A patent family (US 9277002, US 9584389) has a failure term for migrated VMs, which I have seen only in excerpts. Protean's preference rules also need checking.
