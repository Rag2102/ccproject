New: avoiding risky hosts beat every migration rule · exploratory

Move the right VMsbefore they fail.

A cloud can live-migrate VMs off a server that is about to fail, but every move costs bandwidth and a pause, and the budget is finite. This project asks which VMs are worth moving: a policy that weighs failure probability, time to failure, criticality and migration cost, against the habit of moving anything above a probability threshold.

A migration policy that weighs before it moves. It compares the expected cost of leaving a VM where it is with the cost of moving it, per GB moved, and it is judged by a rule fixed before any result was seen.

FIG 0.1

Waiting has a cost

The chance a host fails within 30 days, times what an outage of this VM costs, is the price of leaving it there. That is weighed against the price of moving it, not against a fixed threshold.

FIG 0.2

Benefit per GB, not a count

A live migration costs bandwidth and a pause that grow with memory. Under a finite budget the policy ranks VMs by expected benefit per GB moved; the theory shows its edge over a threshold comes from how much that cost varies.

FIG 0.3

Thirty seeds, one rule

Every comparison was registered before the runs: 30 evaluation seeds, intervals on every number, Holm's correction across the family, and the bar for a win fixed in advance.

Where it sits. It reads what a fleet already records, and decides only through the cost of waiting against the cost of moving.

    Words used on this site

    What each model and each number means, in one or two lines.

    Failure model
    Predicts the chance a drive fails soon. v2 uses 20 gradient-boosted trees trained on Backblaze SMART telemetry; v1 used drive age and model only.
    p (fail within 30 days)
    The model's probability that this drive fails in the next 30 days, with the spread between the 20 members as its uncertainty.
    SMART counters
    Self-monitoring numbers a drive reports: reallocated, pending and uncorrectable sectors, command timeouts. Rising counts are the strongest warning sign.
    Time to failure
    Expected and median days until failure, from p under a constant-hazard assumption. Sooner means more urgent.
    Criticality
    How much an outage of this VM hurts: high, medium or low. It scales the cost of a failure.
    Pre-copy live migration
    Moving a running VM: copy its memory while it runs, then pause briefly to copy what changed. Bigger VMs take longer and cost more.
    Cost of waiting
    p × the cost of a failure for this criticality × urgency, in impact-minutes: what leaving the VM in place is expected to cost.
    Cost of moving
    What the migration itself costs: the pause users feel plus the bandwidth and CPU it uses, in the same impact-minutes.
    Threshold τ
    The common rule: move when p is above a fixed number ({ '%.2f'|format(tau_default * 100) }% here), whatever the VM's size or importance.
    Cost-sensitive policy
    Moves a VM only when waiting is expected to cost more than moving. This project asks when that beats the threshold.
    Budget-aware ranking (BAM)
    The v2 policy: with a limited migration budget, move the VMs with the most expected benefit per GB first.
    Pre-registered
    The comparisons, seeds and the bar for a win were written down before the runs, so the results cannot be tuned after the fact.

    What v2 found

    The −3.1% was re-examined in Week 3, before any v2 runs.

    via docs/v2/v1_anomaly.md

    1. v1 · the headline

      At random placement the cost-sensitive policy cost 3.1% more than a threshold with a matched budget.

    2. Week 3 · the diagnosis

      Mostly a counting effect: the threshold got 8% more migrations, and every migration was credited with failures a median 170 days later, while the decision used a 30-day probability.

    3. Corrected accounting

      With the budget matched and credit limited to the decision's own window, the cost-sensitive rule is ahead by about 0.2%.

    4. Theory and gate G1

      The v2 theory predicts no break-even near ρ ≈ 0.3: the advantage comes from the spread of costs and is largest near ρ = 0. Real placement in three Google cells sits at ρ ≈ 0, the regime that matters.

    A simulator that reproduces v1 exactly

    A day-stepped fleet simulator with VM churn, live-migration queues and repairs reproduces v1's headline costs to a relative error of 6×10⁻⁷ and passes analytic, calibration and invariant checks (gate G2).

    Simulated · pre-registered
    +5% to +11%

    Budget-aware migration beats doing nothing

    Week 18, 30 seeds per cell: BAM saves 5–11% against never migrating in every A regime. The industry-style threshold (P2) costs 2–4× more than doing nothing, so "beating P2", the registered test, says little on its own.

    RESULTS_W18.md · ADR-005
    Simulated
    σκ drives it

    The theory's direction holds in simulation

    At zero correlation, BAM goes from −2.4% to +1.6% to +5.3% against never migrating as the spread of VM costs rises (σ = 0.25, 0.5, 1.0): the first simulation evidence for the theory's claim.

    RESULTS_W18.md · regime E
    Exploratory · 30 seeds
    +11% to +20%

    Where new VMs go matters more than which to move

    Keeping new VMs off the riskiest 2% of hosts, with no migrations at all, beats every migration policy tested, in every regime and every seed. It is why the Narya-inspired policy (P5) beats BAM; adding migration on top gains at most about 2 points.

    RESULTS_AVOID.md · ADR-010
    Simulated · pre-registered
    −30% to −51%

    The safety guard, as registered, does harm

    It ignored pure noise in the smoke test (0 of 5 switches), but its fallback is the threshold P2, so in Week 18 it costs more than never migrating. With a do-nothing fallback it saves 3–9% in regime A (exploratory).
