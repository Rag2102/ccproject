\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Failure prediction.}
Field studies established that drive replacement rates far exceed datasheet values and that
SMART attributes carry real but incomplete signal~\cite{schroeder2007disk,pinheiro2007failure}.
Later work predicts disk replacement from SMART data~\cite{botezatu2016predicting} and, in
production, ranks disks by error-proneness from SMART and system signals with a cost-sensitive
loss, acting on the top~$r$ under capacity constraints~\cite{xu2018cdef}. We use a predictor of
this kind as a fixed input and do not claim a better one. These papers stop at the prediction or
name VM migration as a downstream use; none asks which VMs to move under a migration budget, or
how the answer depends on where critical work sits.

\paragraph{Failure mitigation systems.}
Narya~\cite{levy2020narya} chooses mitigation actions per flagged node with A/B tests and a
Thompson-sampling bandit, NENYA~\cite{wang2022nenya} uses cascade reinforcement learning to trade
mitigation cost against failures for databases, and Deoxys~\cite{zhang2024deoxys} recommends
actions for unhealthy nodes with causal inference. All three are deployed at one operator and
report large relative gains; none publishes data, code or absolute numbers, and none compares
against a probability threshold tuned to the same budget. Protean~\cite{hadary2020protean} avoids
risky hosts at allocation time; we take placement as given and measure it instead. Our P5 is a
Narya-inspired baseline whose every departure from the paper is listed in the artifact. What this
group does not do is isolate the ranking question, bound the gain from workload awareness, or let
anyone reproduce the comparison.

\paragraph{Cost-sensitive learning and decision theory.}
Acting when expected cost exceeds a threshold is the classical cost-sensitive
rule~\cite{elkan2001foundations}, and filling a budget by benefit density is the greedy solution
of the knapsack relaxation~\cite{dantzig1957discrete}. We claim neither. What we add is the
analysis of \emph{when} density ranking beats ranking by probability alone: a closed form for the
advantage as a function of the correlation between cost and risk, the spread of cost per unit of
budget and calibration error, and the observation that the accounting window must match the
decision horizon for the classical guarantee to hold.

\paragraph{Simulators, traces and benchmarks.}
General cloud simulators such as CloudSim Plus~\cite{cloudsimplus} and OpenDC~\cite{opendc} inject
host faults and model migration, and learning-augmented systems platforms such as
Park~\cite{mao2019park} and Gymnasium~\cite{towers2024gymnasium} standardise environments for
learned policies; VM rescheduling has recently been studied with deep reinforcement learning on
production traces~\cite{ding2025vm2rl}, and Virt-LM benchmarks migration mechanisms
themselves~\cite{huang2011virtlm}. Public traces from Google~\cite{tirmazi2020borg,googleclusterdata2019},
Azure~\cite{cortez2017resource,azurepublicdataset} and Backblaze~\cite{backblaze2026drivestats}
make realistic inputs available. We found no benchmark that combines calibrated failure worlds
built from public failure data, a migration budget shared by all moves, paired comparisons on
common random numbers and a frozen protocol, which is what VMMBench provides. Following
holistic-evaluation practice~\cite{liang2023helm}, each scenario is documented by a card and every
reported number traces to a file.
