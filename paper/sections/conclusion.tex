\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked when ranking VMs by expected benefit per GB beats a tuned probability threshold under a
daily migration budget. The answer has three parts. In real placement, as far as public traces
show, critical work is uncorrelated with machine risk ($|\tau_b| \le \VGOneMaxAbsTauB$). That does
not doom workload awareness: under calibrated probabilities and horizon-matched accounting the
budget-aware rule cannot lose in expectation, and its advantage is driven by the spread of cost per
GB rather than by correlation. In a validated simulator at the measured correlation, its adjusted
advantage over a threshold tuned to the same budget is \VDeltaPFourBOneTwo\% at a 2\% budget, and
\VWins{} of \VFamilySize{} pre-registered comparisons clear the 1\% bar.

What we did not show: anything about production performance, about failures other than drives,
or about schedulers other than Borg. The next steps are measured migration costs on a testbed,
