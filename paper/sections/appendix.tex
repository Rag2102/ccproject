\section{Power pilot and scale}
\label{app:pilot}
Before the protocol froze, a pilot on seeds that are used nowhere else (2,000 hosts, 365 days
plus a 30-day warm-up, a 2\% budget) measured the seed-to-seed spread of the paired difference
$\Delta$ between the v1 rule P3 and the tuned threshold P2, as a proxy for every confirmatory
comparison. Its standard deviation was \VPilotSDAThree\% in A3 and \VPilotSDBOne\% in B1. The
protocol requires it to sit below the Bonferroni column for the chosen number of seeds (1.6\% at
\VEvalSeeds{} seeds, 2.1\% at 50), so the matrix runs \VEvalSeeds{} evaluation seeds on
\VHosts{} hosts, with a rule fixed in advance: any regime whose observed spread exceeds the
\VEvalSeeds-seed bound is extended to 50 seeds for every policy and decided against the 50-seed
bound. The rule depends on the spread only, never on the sign or size of $\Delta$. In the main
run it was triggered for A3 and A5 by the bandit and the guard, whose per-seed results vary
more than BAM's. Those regimes are reported at 50 seeds.

\section{The tuned threshold's within-host order}
\label{app:p2order}
P2 moves every VM on a host whose failure probability clears its threshold $\tau$, in some order,
until the day's budget is spent. It is tuned per regime and budget on development seeds to use
about 95\% of the budget, as the protocol defines it, choosing both $\tau$ and the order (smallest
VM first, largest first or random). In the pilot the three orders reached \VPTwoUtilLo--\VPTwoUtilHi\%
utilisation at the same $\tau$ and their total costs differed by at most \VPTwoOrderSpread\%
across \VPTwoOrderCells{} cells, so the order is immaterial to the comparison. Tuning P2 on cost
instead of on the budget was considered and rejected before any evaluation run, because it would
have changed a pre-registered rule after seeing pilot results; the cost of that choice is that
P2 costs more than never migrating, which every results table shows next to it.

\section{How P5 differs from Narya}
\label{app:narya}
P5 is inspired by Narya~\cite{levy2020narya}, not a re-implementation: Narya runs on proprietary
telemetry with unpublished rules and thresholds. Table~\ref{tab:narya} lists the differences that
matter for the comparison. Two of them shape the results. First, P5 can mark a host so that it
takes no new VMs (Narya's \emph{unallocatable} action), which no other policy can do; the
exploratory diagnosis in Section~\ref{sec:eval} attributes P5's whole advantage over BAM to it.
Second, P5 learns online within each run, so its first weeks are exploration.

\section{Robustness grid}
\label{app:robust}
The full grid of the robustness study (Section~\ref{sec:eval}) varies, one at a time and then
jointly in a Morris screening, the cost-model estimation error, miscalibration, predictor noise,
the non-disk hazard, repair time, unmitigated downtime, concurrent migrations per host and the
initial fill. The two factors with the largest effect on BAM's advantage are the
\VMorrisTopFactor{} and the \VMorrisSecondFactor. The per-factor table is generated from the
Week 20 summaries and appears here once they exist.
