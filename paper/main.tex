\title{Which VMs to Move Before the Hardware Fails:\\Budget-Aware Migration, Measured and Bounded}
\author{Anonymous}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
% 150-200 words (plan Week 23); every number is a macro; every sentence maps to the claims ledger
Cloud operators move VMs off hosts predicted to fail, under a daily migration budget. Which VMs
should go first? Ranking by expected benefit per GB moved is a classical cost-sensitive rule, but
when it beats the operational default, a tuned threshold on failure probability, has not been
measured, and our own prototype suggested it pays only when critical VMs sit on risky hosts. We
show that this was an accounting artifact. In three Google 2019 cells, criticality and machine risk
are essentially uncorrelated ($|\tau_b| \le \VGOneMaxAbsTauB$). Under calibrated probabilities,
equal budgets and horizon-matched accounting, budget-aware ranking never loses in expectation; a
closed form, confirmed by Monte Carlo at \VTheoryPoints{} points, shows its advantage is driven by
the spread of cost per GB, not by correlation, while flattening miscalibration can reverse it. In
a validated simulator built from public drive, VM and placement data, at the measured
correlation and a 2\% budget, budget-aware migration's adjusted advantage over the tuned threshold
is \VDeltaPFourBOneTwo\% (95\% CI [\VCILoPFourBOneTwo, \VCIHiPFourBOneTwo]\%), and \VWins{} of
\VFamilySize{} pre-registered comparisons clear a 1\% bar. We release the simulator, the frozen
protocol and VMMBench.
\end{abstract}
