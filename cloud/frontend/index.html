Under random workload placement, the two policies land within ~3% of each other on total cost (matched to the same migration budget) -- an honest, non-confirmatory baseline result. The follow-up experiment on the right shows where the hypothesis does hold.

Does a deep attention model beat the calibrated hazard model?

Both models scored on the identical held-out test rows -- including why a deep attention architecture doesn't beat a well-calibrated gradient-boosted model here.

ModelROC-AUCPR-AUCBrierECE

The gradient-boosted hazard model wins on every metric, especially calibration -- consistent with the well-known finding that tree-based models tend to match or beat deep architectures on small-feature-count tabular problems, and a reminder that a fancier architecture doesn't automatically produce a usable probability.
