The project's real findings precomputed, from the actual experiments

These numbers come from the full replay simulation (task 8) and the correlation-sweep follow-up (task 9) over the real Backblaze + Azure datasets -- not from this demo's live calculator above.

Under random workload placement, the two policies land within ~3% of each other on total cost (matched to the same migration budget) -- the honest, non-confirmatory task 8 result. The follow-up experiment below shows where the hypothesis does hold.

Task 11: does a deep attention model beat the calibrated hazard model?

Both models scored on the identical held-out test rows. See docs/task11_summary.md for the full writeup, including why TensorFlow/PyTorch aren't used here.

ModelROC-AUCPR-AUCBrierECE

The gradient-boosted hazard model wins on every metric, especially calibration -- consistent with the well-known finding that tree-based models tend to match or beat deep architectures on small-feature-count tabular problems, and a reminder that a fancier architecture doesn't automatically produce a usable probability.
